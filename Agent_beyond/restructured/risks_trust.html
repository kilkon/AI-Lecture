Risk and Trust

Risks, Limits, and Trust

Trust cannot be treated as a compliance appendix. In agent-based systems, trust is an operational requirement that shapes deployment scope, governance design, and human willingness to rely on the system.

1. Reliability and performance

  • Hallucinations, brittle reasoning, and weak context retention.
  • Silent failure modes in multi-agent handoffs and tool use.
  • Performance drift as policies, interfaces, and data change over time.

2. Human agency and labor quality

  • Deskilling when people become passive reviewers of machine output.
  • Automation pressure on clerical and support roles.
  • Fatigue and confusion if escalation paths are unclear.

3. Governance, legal, and data exposure

  • Privacy, confidentiality, procurement, and IP risks.
  • Weak accountability when multiple vendors and agents are involved.
  • Regulatory mismatch between fast experimentation and slow oversight.

4. Equity, legitimacy, and public trust

  • Bias amplification across admissions, grading, HR, or service delivery.
  • Opaque ranking or routing systems that affect people unequally.
  • Loss of institutional legitimacy when appeal channels are weak.

What trust requires in practice

Testing before deployment Monitoring after deployment Clear escalation paths Human override rights Documented accountability Periodic red-team review

Core leadership question

The hardest question is not "Can the agent do this?" but "Who is accountable when it fails, and how quickly can the institution recover trust?"
